# TOD Policy Radius Comparison Analysis

## Executive Summary

Comparison of three policy scenarios for Transit-Oriented Development zones around BSL/MFL stations, analyzing development captured at different radii.

---

## Three Scenarios Analyzed

### **SCENARIO 1: 500 Feet (Current City TOD Overlays)**
- **Radius**: 500 feet (152 meters)
- **Status**: Already exists for 13 MFL stations
- **Captures**: ~1,300 units (49 permits)
- **Political ease**: ⭐ Easiest (already law)

### **SCENARIO 2: 1/4 Mile (Mayor's Proposal)**  
- **Radius**: 1/4 mile / 1,320 feet (402 meters)
- **Status**: Mayor has called for this
- **Captures**: ~5,000 units (291 permits)
- **Political ease**: ⭐⭐ Moderate (mayoral backing)
- **Gain vs 500ft**: +3,700 units (+283%)

### **SCENARIO 3: 1/2 Mile (Standard TOD / State Bill)**
- **Radius**: 1/2 mile / 2,640 feet (805 meters)
- **Status**: Table preference, standard TOD planning distance, state bill coverage
- **Captures**: ~17,000 units (990 permits)
- **Political ease**: ⭐⭐⭐ Hardest locally, easier at state
- **Gain vs 1/4mi**: +12,000 units (+240%)
- **Gain vs 500ft**: +15,700 units (+1,205%)

## Strategic Options

### **Option A: Expand Existing TOD Overlays (Easiest)**
**Argument**: "Just expand the 13 stations that already have TOD zoning from 500 feet to 1/4 mile"

**Advantages**:
- Precedent already set (these zones exist)
- Mayor supports 1/4 mile
- Smaller geographic expansion
- Only affects MFL corridor (Districts 3, 4, 6, 7)

**Captures**: 477 units (just the 13 stations)

### **Option B: 1/4 Mile at ALL BSL/MFL Stations (Moderate)**
**Argument**: "Apply the mayor's proposed 1/4 mile distance to all rapid transit stations"

**Advantages**:
- Mayoral backing for distance
- Logical extension (why only some stations?)
- Includes BSL (adds Districts 1, 2, 5, 8, 9)
- Captures where development is actually happening

**Captures**: ~5,000 units (all 30 BSL/MFL stations)

### **Option C: State Bill - 1/2 Mile at ALL SEPTA Stations (Best Coverage)**
**Argument**: "State-level reform captures 3-4x more development, avoids hostile local councils"

**Advantages**:
- Standard TOD planning distance (established practice)
- Bypasses anti-development local councils
- Includes trolleys, regional rail (massive additional coverage)
- Where your political energy is already focused

**Captures**: ~17,000 units (BSL/MFL alone) + trolleys + regional rail

## Political Landscape Analysis

### **Key Challenge**: Best stations have worst politics
- **District 5**: Both Girards + Erie = 2,029 units, but **anti-development council member**
- **District 7**: York-Dauphin + Berks = top 2 citywide, but **anti-development council member**
- **Districts 1, 3**: More moderate, but less development

### **Counter-Argument for Districts 5 & 7**:
> "Your districts already have explosive growth (York-Dauphin up 649%, Berks up 110%). The market is building here regardless of zoning. TOD policy doesn't CAUSE development - it MANAGES it and ensures affordability benefits reach residents. Without TOC rules, you get market-rate luxury with no community benefit."

---

## Recommended Messaging by Scenario

### **For Local Council (if pursuing locally)**:
1. **Start with existing overlays**: "13 MFL stations already have TOD zones - just expand them from 500ft to 1/4 mile like the mayor proposed"
2. **If that works, expand coverage**: "Now apply the same 1/4 mile to BSL stations too"
3. **Use development data**: "These areas are building anyway - let's get affordability requirements"

### **For State Bill (current strategy)**:
1. **Emphasize local obstruction**: "Cities like Philadelphia won't act because the best development areas have anti-development local councilmembers"
2. **Show what's possible**: "1/2 mile at BSL/MFL alone captures 17,000 units - 3x more than local 1/4 mile proposal"
3. **Add trolleys & regional rail**: "Plus 551 trolley stops and regional rail = tens of thousands more units"
