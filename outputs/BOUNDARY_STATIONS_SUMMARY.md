# BOUNDARY STATIONS REQUIRING JOINT COUNCIL DECISIONS

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**21 transit stations** (32% of all stations analyzed) sit on or within 100 meters of council district boundaries. These require **joint approval** from neighboring council members.

**CRITICAL FINDING:** 6 of the top 10 citywide priority stations are boundary cases, including:
- **Ellsworth-Federal** (BSL, #6 citywide, Tier 1) - Districts 1/2
- **Huntingdon** (MFL, #8 citywide, Tier 3) - Districts 1/7
- **Somerset** (MFL, #10 citywide, Tier 3) - Districts 1/7

**Political Implication:** Any district-by-district approach (Plan B) requires coordination between multiple council members for the highest-priority stations. This strengthens the case for a **citywide ordinance** (Plan A) rather than piecemeal district opt-ins.

## POLITICAL STRATEGY FOR BOUNDARY STATIONS

### **Key Insight: Boundary Stations Favor Citywide Approach**

The fact that **21 stations (32% of total)** require joint decisions between council members is a **strategic argument FOR a citywide ordinance** rather than district-by-district opt-in.

**Arguments:**

1. **Simplicity:** Citywide rule avoids 21 separate inter-district negotiations
2. **Equity:** Ensures neighboring districts don't block each other's development opportunities
3. **Consistency:** Same rules apply regardless of which side of the street you're on
4. **Speed:** Single citywide vote faster than coordinating multiple district pairs

### **Prioritization for District-by-District Fallback (Plan B)**

If forced to pursue district-by-district approach, prioritize boundary stations as follows:

**Tier 1 Boundary Priorities (Top 10 Citywide + Boundary):**
1. **Ellsworth-Federal** (D1/D2) - #6 citywide, 452 units
2. **Girard MFL** (D1/D5) - #5 citywide, 816 units
3. **Huntingdon** (D1/D7) - #8 citywide, 156 units
4. **Somerset** (D1/D7) - #10 citywide, 50 units

**Tier 2 Boundary Priorities (Strong Candidates + Boundary):**
5. **Erie BSL** (D5/D8) - #7 citywide, 255 units

**Tier 3 Boundary Priorities (Moderate/Lower):**
- All other boundary stations (56th, 60th, 63rd, Allegheny BSL, etc.)

### **Messaging for Boundary Stations**

**Frame as Collaborative Opportunity:**
- "Joint TOC designation shows neighboring districts working together for citywide housing goals"
- "Development doesn't respect district boundaries—neither should our zoning policy"
- "Shared transit corridors = shared economic opportunity"

**Emphasize Existing Development:**
- "These stations already have [X] units built—TOC formalizes what the market has validated"
- "Residents on both sides of [District X/Y boundary] deserve same access to transit-oriented housing"

**Use Boundary Complexity to Argue for Citywide Ordinance:**
- "Rather than negotiate 21 separate boundary agreements, a citywide ordinance treats all stations equally"
- "Simple, uniform rules prevent confusion and delays"
